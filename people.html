LITERACY FOR PEOPLE

Digital skills should include everyone.

People use technology to learn, communicate, access services, solve problems, and express themselves. Digital literacy supports confidence, safety, and responsible participation.

Key areas of digital citizenship

Understanding these areas helps you participate positively and safely in the digital world.

Digital communication

Email, messaging, video calls, and social media all require clarity, timing, and tone. Good communication online means being understood while respecting others.

Digital citizenship

Respect others, protect privacy, understand the consequences of your actions, and contribute positively to online communities.

Accessibility & inclusion

Clear language, readable interfaces, assistive technologies, and inclusive design help more people participate fully in digital life.

Building a positive online presence

Your online identity and wellbeing are important. Here is how to manage them thoughtfully.

Online identity

What you share online creates a lasting impression. Be thoughtful about what you post, what profile information is visible, and how others may perceive you.

Digital wellbeing

Balanced technology use includes managing screen time, recognising when content affects your mood, and knowing when to disconnect and rest.

Interactive scenarios

Practise making good decisions in realistic digital situations.

Scenario: Unexpected message

You receive a message from someone you do not know asking for personal information. What should you do?

Correct. Never share personal information with unknown contacts. Legitimate organisations will not ask for sensitive data through direct messages. Blocking and reporting helps protect you and others.

Scenario: Social media post

A friend shares an embarrassing photo of someone without their permission. What should you do?

Correct. Sharing or encouraging the spread of non-consensual images can cause real harm. Respecting others' dignity online is a core part of digital citizenship.

Scenario: Workplace message

You receive an urgent email appearing to be from your manager, asking you to buy gift cards for a client. What should you do?

Correct. Urgency and unusual requests are common signs of impersonation. Always verify unexpected requests through a known, trusted channel before taking action.
